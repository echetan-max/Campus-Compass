How Classes & Labs Work

01Theory classes are held in lecture halls — 1-hour sessions, multiple per day. Bring your notebook, your laptop if needed, and wake up early (first class is usually 8 AM!).
02Lab sessions are 3-hour practical blocks. You'll do programming labs, circuit labs, simulation work. Prepare the pre-lab questions beforehand — most faculty check!
03Tutorials are smaller sessions for solving problems and doubts. Actually attend these — they directly help in exams and CIA components.
0475% attendance is mandatory per subject. Below 75% = detained from exam. Track attendance on the ERP portal daily — don't let it sneak up on you.
05Sit in the front rows for the first few weeks — you'll get noticed positively by faculty, which matters when attendance borderline issues come up.

Exam Pattern — Know This Cold

CIA — Continuous Internal Assessment
40%

Multiple tests throughout the semester + assignments + lab records + viva. CIA is your chance to build a cushion before the final exam. Never skip CIA prep — it's more forgiving than ESE.


  • → CIA 1 & CIA 2 (written tests)
  • → Lab records & lab exams
  • → Assignments & project work
  • → Attendance component
ESE — End Semester Exam
60%

A single 3-hour exam at the end of the semester covering the entire syllabus. It's high stakes but manageable if you've kept up with the subject. Previous year papers are gold.


  • → Covers all units (typically 5 units)
  • → 3-hour duration
  • → Standardized question pattern
  • → Minimum 40% needed to pass

🎯 CGPA Tips from Day 1

→Score well in CIA — 40% of marks are earned before the final exam. Never treat CIA as unimportant.
→Make a subject-wise timetable from Week 2. Don't wait until before exams to start revising.
→Form study groups with 3–4 serious peers. Teaching someone else is the best way to learn.
→Download previous year question papers from the library portal. Most questions repeat in pattern.
→A 8+ CGPA in Semester 1 sets a strong foundation. It's easier to maintain than recover.
→NPTEL online courses complement your syllabus and can earn you extra credits. Start from Sem 2.

How to Get Into Clubs

📅Club Recruitment Week happens in the first month of college — usually during orientation. Every club sets up a stall on campus. Walk up, talk to members, show genuine interest.
📝Application forms are either physical or via Google Forms shared on notice boards and WhatsApp groups. Fill them carefully and honestly — clubs value authenticity over polished answers.
🎤Interviews / auditions vary by club. Technical clubs test aptitude and enthusiasm. Arts clubs may have auditions. Don't overthink it — freshers are expected to be beginners.
💡Pro tip: Apply to 2–3 clubs you genuinely care about rather than all of them. Quality involvement > resume padding. Clubs value consistent members over one-time participants.

Why Clubs Matter for Your Career

🚀Clubs give you real projects to talk about in interviews — things that textbooks don't. A hackathon project you built at Chakravyuha beats a generic assignment every time.
🤝Networking through clubs connects you to alumni who are now at top companies, startups, and research labs. These connections are invaluable for internships and placements.
🏆Club leadership roles (team lead, coordinator, president) look excellent on a resume and develop skills that no classroom teaches — communication, conflict resolution, ownership.

Campus & Hostel Life

A typical day at Amrita Amaravati — what to expect, where to go, and how to actually enjoy it.

Your Typical Day as a Fresher

5:30 – 6:30 AMWake up, freshen up, morning exercise or walk on campus. The campus is beautiful in the early morning — enjoy the quiet before the chaos.
7:00 – 7:30 AMHostel mess breakfast. Common options: idli/dosa, bread-butter, fruits. Eat well — skipping breakfast before a 3-hour class is painful.
8:00 AM – 1:00 PMMorning classes — typically 3–4 lectures or a lab session. Attendance is marked. Stay engaged, sit up front, ask questions.
1:00 – 2:00 PMLunch break at the mess or campus canteen. This is prime time to meet batchmates, seniors, and club members. Don't eat alone every day.
2:00 – 5:00 PMAfternoon classes, practicals, or tutorials. Sometimes free slots — use them in the library or working on assignments rather than just scrolling phones.
5:00 – 7:00 PMClub activities, sports, or personal projects. This is your creative time — protect it. Don't let it become purely Netflix time every day.
7:30 – 8:30 PMDinner at the mess. Evening snacks at canteen. A common social hour for most students.
9:00 – 11:00 PMStudy time. Review the day's classes, complete assignments, prepare pre-lab. 2 hours of focused study > 5 hours of distracted studying.
11:00 PMLights out (ideally). 7–8 hours of sleep is non-negotiable for sharp cognitive function. Don't fall into the "sleep 4 hours" culture — it's not sustainable.

Survival Kit

Hard-earned wisdom from seniors who've made every mistake in the book so you don't have to.

✅ DO These
  • Always carry your college ID card everywhere on campus
  • Maintain 85%+ attendance buffer (not just 75%)
  • Submit assignments before the deadline, not at 11:59 PM
  • Talk to your faculty — they're more approachable than you think
  • Backup your work on Google Drive or GitHub regularly
  • Sleep at least 7 hours — your brain needs it
  • Join at least one club and actually show up
  • Call home regularly — parents worry, and it keeps you grounded
  • Keep a weekly planner for deadlines and events
  • Be curious — ask questions in class and online
❌ DON'T Do These
  • Don't bunk classes thinking you'll "catch up later" — you won't
  • Don't pull all-nighters regularly — it kills productivity for 3 days
  • Don't compare your progress to others' highlight reels on Instagram
  • Don't wait until CIA week to open your textbooks for the first time
  • Don't ignore homesickness — it's normal and passes with time
  • Don't take on too many commitments in Semester 1 — find your pace first
  • Don't hesitate to ask for help — academically or personally
  • Don't lose your original admit documents — keep them safe always
  • Don't trust "attendance proxy" stories — it's not worth the risk
  • Don't eat only canteen junk food every day — balance your diet

Common Mistakes Seniors Made (Learn From Us 😅)

01
Ignoring CIA Preparation
"It's just an internal test" they said. Then they needed 95% in ESE to pass. CIA is 40% of your grade — treat every test seriously.
02
Joining Too Many Clubs
Saying yes to 6 clubs in Week 1 and contributing to none is worse than being in no club. Pick 2, show up consistently, grow within them.
03
Skipping Orientation Events
Orientation week is how you meet your lifelong college friends. The seniors who guide you become your network. Don't skip these events to "rest".
04
Not Learning Basic Linux/Git Early
CSE students especially — not knowing git by Semester 2 makes lab assignments and projects 3x harder. Learn the basics in your first month.
05
Procrastinating on Internship Prep
Internship season hits fast. Students who start DSA practice in Semester 2 have a massive edge over those who start in Semester 5.
06
Not Using the Library
The library has textbooks, reference books, and digital journals that cost thousands outside. Students who use it consistently outperform those who rely only on PDFs.

Time Management & Balance

⏰Use the 2-hour rule: spend 2 hours reviewing every day's classes in the evening. This alone eliminates most last-minute exam panic.
📋Maintain a weekly to-do list every Sunday. List assignments, tests, club events, and personal goals. Review it each evening — it takes 5 minutes and saves hours of "what should I do now?"
🎯Use the Pomodoro technique for studying: 25 minutes focused work, 5-minute break, repeat. After 4 cycles, take a longer 20-minute break. It's surprisingly effective.
🚫Phone-free study sessions: Put your phone in another room or use apps like Forest when studying. Even 90 minutes of truly focused study beats 4 hours of half-distracted studying.
🧠 Mental Health Matters

Transitioning to college away from home is a massive change. It's completely normal to feel overwhelmed, homesick, or anxious in the first few weeks — and even beyond that. You're not alone.

  • Talk to someone you trust — a friend, senior, or faculty advisor
  • Amrita has a student counselling cell — use it without hesitation
  • Exercise regularly — even 20-minute walks make a real difference
  • Keep a journal — writing your thoughts out reduces anxiety
  • Limit social media comparison — everyone else is also figuring things out
  • Maintain a connection with home — call family at least twice a week
🌟 Signs You're Doing Great
  • You know your class timetable and where your classes are
  • You have at least one person you can eat lunch with
  • You're attending more than 80% of your classes
  • You've attended at least one club event or workshop
  • You've explored the campus beyond your hostel and classrooms
  • You feel slightly less confused than the week before

Progress, not perfection. College is a marathon, not a sprint.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions every fresher has but is too nervous to ask seniors. We asked them for you.

What should I bring on my first day to college? +
Bring your original admission documents and multiple photocopies, passport photos (10+), a laptop (if you have one), basic stationery (notebooks, pens, highlighters), and a willingness to walk a lot! Don't over-pack on day 1 — you'll figure out what you need after settling in. Also bring your bedding, basic medicines, and a power strip with multiple sockets for your room.
Is the hostel life strict? Are there curfew timings? +
Yes, there are rules and timings. Boys' and girls' hostels both have entry/exit timings — typically gates close around 9–10 PM on weekdays. Weekends may have slightly later timings for sanctioned events. Rule-following in the first semester builds trust with wardens, and that trust gives you more flexibility over time. The rules exist for safety — respect them.
What happens if my attendance drops below 75%? +
If your attendance falls below 75% in a subject, you may be detained from appearing in that subject's End Semester Exam. This means you'll have to repeat the course the following year, which directly impacts your CGPA and graduation timeline. Medical leave applications can sometimes help, but approval isn't guaranteed. The simple answer: don't let it happen. Track your attendance on ERP weekly.
Can first-year students go out on weekends? +
Yes, with proper outpass procedures. You typically need to submit an outpass request to the hostel warden (sometimes with parental permission initially). Day outpass lets you return by evening; overnight outpass has stricter approval requirements. As you build a track record of following rules, this process becomes smoother. Vijayawada is nearby (30 mins) and has malls, markets, and restaurants to explore.
Which laptop should a CSE fresher buy? +
Aim for a laptop with at least 8GB RAM (16GB preferred), an SSD (512 GB minimum), and a decent processor (AMD Ryzen 5 or Intel i5 and above). MacBooks are popular for their reliability and Unix-like environment but aren't necessary. Brands like ASUS, Lenovo ThinkPad, and Dell Inspiron offer solid options between ₹50,000–₹80,000. Avoid gaming laptops for academic use — they're heavy and battery life is poor.
Is ragging an issue? How safe is the campus? +
Amrita has a strict zero-tolerance policy on ragging. There's an anti-ragging committee, and complaints are taken seriously. The campus is well-monitored with security at gates and throughout campus. You should feel safe. If you ever feel uncomfortable or witness any inappropriate behaviour toward yourself or others, report it immediately to the anti-ragging helpline or your faculty advisor. The college takes this very seriously.
What coding languages should I learn first? +
Start with C (your first semester will likely cover it), then move to Python in parallel — it's versatile for everything from web development to AI. Java is also part of the curriculum. Don't try to learn everything at once. C teaches you memory and fundamentals; Python teaches you to build fast. Once you're comfortable with one, picking up others becomes exponentially easier. DSA in C++ is the standard for competitive programming and placements.
How do I prepare for placements from Day 1? +
The honest answer: maintain a good CGPA (most companies filter at 7.0+), learn DSA consistently (start LeetCode easy problems in Sem 2), build at least 2–3 real projects by Sem 5, do a summer internship (aim for one after Sem 4), and stay active on LinkedIn and GitHub. Students who start early have a massive advantage. Don't let placement panic hit you in Sem 6 when everyone else starts. Start now, go slow, stay consistent.
What if I chose the "wrong" branch? Can I change? +
Branch changes are possible at Amrita but typically only at the end of Semester 1 based on academic performance and seat availability. The process is competitive. However, the bigger truth: your branch matters much less than your skills and projects. CSE concepts like DSA, databases, and software development are learnable by any branch — and many non-CSE students land excellent tech roles. Focus on building skills, not lamenting your branch.
